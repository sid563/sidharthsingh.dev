Every distributed systems course starts from the inside. Here is a node, here is a message, here is
the moment the message is late. It is a reasonable place to start and a terrible place to stop,
because nobody who runs a system ever sees it from there. You see a dashboard. You see a queue that
has started growing at a steady angle, or a p99 that has gone from flat to a staircase, or a service
that reports healthy while doing nothing at all.

> Experience is not having seen the failure. It is recognising it from the shape it leaves on a
> graph you were not even looking at.

## Silhouettes

A partition and a slow disk look nothing alike from inside the system and almost identical from
outside: both produce timeouts, both produce retries, both make the retry storm look like the cause.
What separates the engineer who fixes it in ten minutes from the one who spends the evening is not
theory. It is having a small catalogue of silhouettes and the discipline to check the cheap ones
first.

## Learning them on purpose

The roadmap has a failure-mode lab on it for exactly this reason: three nodes, a queue, a database, a
cache, and a list of things to break deliberately. Partition the network. Skew a clock. Make one disk
slow. Each time, write down what the outside looked like before writing down what happened inside.

The write-ups will go here, as notes, one failure at a time. The goal is not to be surprised less. It
is to be surprised faster, and then to know which graph to open next.
