For two years most of my production data work was MongoDB: aggregation pipelines, compound indexes, Decimal128 everywhere money shows up. Then the order-fulfilment service I work on landed on PostgreSQL, and I noticed I could write the SQL but could not explain what the planner would do with it.

So I set up an eight-week course for myself, with a tutor that writes the assignments and reviews the notes, and I ordered it internals first:

1. Lab and system catalogs
2. Architecture and the life of a query
3. Storage: pages, tuples, TOAST
4. MVCC and locks
5. Indexes
6. The planner (two weeks)
7. WAL, memory and durability
8. Features worth knowing
9. Observability and operations

The bet is simple. If I understand how a tuple is laid out on a page and how MVCC decides what a transaction can see, then every feature on top of that becomes a consequence rather than a fact to memorise.

Progress is tracked publicly on the [progress page](/progress/postgres-internals). The honest number at the time of writing is low, and that is the point of writing it down.

<Callout type="tip" title="Books">
Egor Rogov's *PostgreSQL 14 Internals* and Hironobu Suzuki's *The Internals of PostgreSQL* are the two core texts.
</Callout>
